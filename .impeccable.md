## Design Context

### Users
Mid-to-senior knowledge workers (engineers, PMs, AI/ML roles) running disciplined, multi-month job searches. They are technically literate — they came to career-ops via a CLI. They use the tool during focused, often-evening sessions, sometimes for hours, frequently triaging dozens of postings in a sitting. They want signal, not noise. They have already opted out of mass-application culture.

### Brand Personality
Opinionated, anti-spray-and-pray, data-forward, slightly subversive. The product's tagline — *"Companies use AI to filter candidates. I just gave candidates AI to choose companies"* — sets the tone: this is a candidate's command center, not another LinkedIn. Three words: **deliberate, instrumented, sharp**.

### Aesthetic Direction
Command-center editorial. Think dim newsroom desk meets Bloomberg terminal: warm-tinted dark surfaces, sparing ember accents for active state, retro-terminal monospace for all numerical data, strong grotesque display for hierarchy. Reject the AI-saas defaults (cyan-on-near-black, purple-blue gradients, glassmorphism, glow). Dense, asymmetric, table-first. The CLI/terminal heritage should be visible without being costume.

**Anti-references:** generic admin dashboards (Material/AntD), Linear-clones, Notion-grey, anything with a sidebar of icons-and-text-on-hover, anything with a hero card grid of "features."

### Design Principles
1. **Data dignifies the user.** Numbers, scores, and statuses get the most typographic weight. Chrome shrinks.
2. **One ember, never a rainbow.** A single warm accent marks active/now. Status colors stay muted (sage, oxblood, amber) so the accent retains power.
3. **Tables, not cards.** Pipeline-shaped data lives in dense rows with monospace alignment. Cards are reserved for genuine container moments.
4. **Terminal heritage, not terminal cosplay.** Departure Mono for data, retro CRT cues are off-limits unless they earn their place.
5. **The user is operating, not browsing.** Keyboard-first affordances are visible at all times (command bar at bottom).
